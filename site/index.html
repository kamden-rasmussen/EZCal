A fast, keyboard-first calendar for people who live in Google Calendar — and iCloud.

EZCal is a native Mac app that puts Google Calendar, Apple Calendar accounts, subscribed .ics feeds and Reminders in one grid. This page walks through what it does, what it deliberately doesn't, and where the rough edges are.

Feature tour

What's in it

Everything below is from the app as it ships. Clips are short and unedited, recorded against built-in demo data.

Day, work week, week and month

Switch views with D 5 W M. The grid opens scrolled to the start of your working day, and hours outside your working hours are shaded. Two-finger swipes glide a day at a time rather than jumping a whole week.

  • Working hours
  • Hide weekends
  • Second time zone gutter

⌘K command palette

Search events, jump to natural-language dates like "next friday", show or hide a coworker, or run any command in the app. It's the fastest way to do almost everything, which is the point.

  • Event search
  • Date jump
  • Every command

Create, edit and RSVP

Click or drag on the grid to create an event at that time, or press N. Save with ⌘↩. Recurring events ask whether a change applies to this event, this and following, or all. Pick Zoom or Google Meet from the editor, and an availability warning appears while you invite people.

  • Recurrence scopes
  • RSVP with Y / M / N
  • Visibility: default · public · private

Move and resize without the mouse

Arrow keys walk between events. ⌥↓ moves the focused event 15 minutes later; ⌥⇧↓ resizes it. With a mouse, drag a block to move it (snapping to 15 minutes, across day columns) or drag its bottom edge to resize. esc cancels a drag mid-gesture.

Coworker overlay

Search your Google Workspace directory, click a person, and their calendar appears in your grid. If they share details you see full events; if only availability is shared you see dashed "Busy" blocks; if nothing is shared you get a warning icon. The same meeting on several calendars is drawn once.

Workspace directory search needs a Workspace account. On personal Gmail you can still add people by email.

Find a time

Press ⌘F in the editor. EZCal lists the best slots where every attendee it can see is free, with a per-day availability grid. People whose calendars aren't shared show as "unknown" and never block a slot, so treat results as a strong suggestion, not a guarantee.

One-click video calls

Every event with a Zoom, Meet or Teams link gets a Join button; J joins the focused event or your next meeting. Zoom meetings are real: created through the Zoom API with your defaults (waiting room, mute on entry, passcode and so on), updated when you move the event, and deleted when you delete it. A menu bar item shows your next meeting, and optional reminders arrive a few minutes before with a Join action.

Google, iCloud and subscribed feeds together

Sign in with multiple Google accounts. Any account macOS already syncs (iCloud, Exchange, Fastmail, CalDAV) appears too, with no passwords or API keys. Paste a webcal:// or https:// link to subscribe to a sports schedule or shared team calendar: read-only, refreshed in the background. Events present on more than one source are de-duplicated.

  • Multiple Google accounts
  • EventKit / Apple Calendar
  • .ics subscriptions

Reminders as tasks, in the grid

Due-dated Apple Reminders appear as chips in the all-day row or small timed markers, with a checkbox to complete them. Quick-add from ⌘K: task: call dentist tomorrow 3pm. There's no task editor; anything beyond create and complete happens in Reminders.app.

Smaller things that add up

  • Private events. Mark an event private and anyone viewing your calendar, including EZCal's overlay, sees only "Busy". Google calendars only.
  • Multiple windows. ⌘⇧N opens another window with its own date and view, handy for comparing two weeks.
  • Light and dark. Follows the system.
  • Update pill. Checks the Homebrew tap for new releases and shows how to upgrade.

Pros & cons

The honest version

Drawn from the project's own documentation of what works and what doesn't.

＋ What it does well

  • Speed and feel. Native SwiftUI, a command palette, and a shortcut for nearly everything.
  • One grid, many sources. Google, iCloud, Exchange, CalDAV and .ics feeds side by side, de-duplicated.
  • Privacy by architecture. No server in the middle. Tokens are in your Keychain; requests go from your Mac to Google or Zoom.
  • Scheduling tools. Coworker overlay, availability warnings and Find a time.
  • Real Zoom integration. Meetings are created, updated and deleted with the event.
  • Free and hackable. MIT, no dependencies, small readable codebase with unit tests.

− Where it falls short

  • Mac only. Requires macOS 14+. An iOS build exists but reads on-device calendars only; Google and Zoom aren't wired up there yet.
  • Apple calendars are second-class for invites. EventKit can't invite people or set your RSVP, so those controls, Google Meet and private events are hidden on iCloud calendars.
  • Credentials. Google and Zoom OAuth clients are compiled into the build. Building it yourself means creating a Google Cloud project (about five minutes).
  • Limited recurrence authoring. Only daily, weekly, monthly and weekdays; no custom rule builder.
  • Read-only feeds are basic. .ics subscriptions skip per-occurrence overrides and some RRULE forms.
  • No task editor. Tasks without a due date don't show in the grid.
  • No moving an event between calendars from the editor.
Full list of known limitations
  • "This and following events" on Google splits the series by truncating the old rule; all-day recurring series' UNTIL isn't handled correctly yet.
  • Deleting less than the whole series never detaches its Zoom meeting.
  • A Google calendar that reaches EZCal only through macOS Internet Accounts has no Visibility control; connect the account directly to mark events private.
  • "Remove Zoom link" can remove more than the Zoom lines when a Google event's description is HTML.
  • Very old Outlook-style time zone names in .ics feeds fall back to your Mac's time zone.
  • A timed task marker doesn't take part in overlap layout, so a busy hour can show it over an event.
  • Multiple windows share accounts and selections but not a live event cache; an edit in one window appears in the other after its next refresh (⌘R).

Install

Getting it running

With Homebrew

brew install --cask kamden-rasmussen/tap/ezcal

Releases are signed and notarized. Update with brew update && brew upgrade --cask ezcal; the app also tells you when a new version is out.

From source

scripts/build_app.sh --run

Needs macOS 14+ and Xcode / Swift 5.9+. For Google or Zoom sign-in you supply your own OAuth client; iCloud and Apple Calendar need no setup at all.

Try it without an account

EZCAL_DEMO=1 build/EZCal.app/Contents/MacOS/EZCal

Demo mode loads fake calendars and events and touches none of your accounts. Every screenshot and clip on this page was recorded this way.

Is it for you?

A quick gut check

Probably a good fit if you…

  • Work mostly on a Mac and in Google Calendar, maybe with iCloud alongside.
  • Prefer keyboards and command palettes to clicking.
  • Schedule with coworkers and want their availability in view.
  • Like open-source tools you can read and change.

Probably not yet if you…

  • Need Windows, Linux or a polished iPhone app.
  • Rely on complex custom recurrence rules.
  • Want to invite people or set RSVPs on iCloud calendars.
  • Don't want to set up credentials and can't use the prebuilt Homebrew build.
